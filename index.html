AI IN AFRICA

Is the continent ready for the AI revolution?

Artificial intelligence is transforming economies, education, healthcare, industry and public administration around the world. However, countries do not enter this technological revolution with the same resources, infrastructure or capabilities.

This project investigates Africa's position in the global AI landscape through data visualization, comparing countries and examining the technological, economic and social conditions that shape AI readiness.

Data and methodology

Why these indicators

AI readiness depends on government capacity, digital infrastructure and people's skills. We use one composite index and a few basic World Bank indicators that cover each of these.

  • Government AI Readiness Index 2025 (Oxford Insights): a 0–100 composite score of how ready a government is to use AI, built from six pillars (policy capacity, AI infrastructure, governance, public-sector adoption, development and diffusion, resilience).
  • Internet users (IT.NET.USER.ZS), electricity access (EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS) and mobile subscriptions (IT.CEL.SETS.P2): basic digital infrastructure.
  • Tertiary enrolment (SE.TER.ENRR), R&D expenditure (GB.XPD.RSDV.GD.ZS) and researchers per million (SP.POP.SCIE.RD.P6): proxies for human capital, not direct measures of AI skills.

How comparisons were made

For each World Bank indicator we use the latest year a country reported between 2000 and 2025; the year is shown in every tooltip. Averages are unweighted means of the countries with data. Correlations are descriptive and do not show cause and effect.

Limitations

  • Readiness is not adoption or impact. The index measures preconditions, not how much AI is actually used or what it changes.
  • Reporting years differ between countries, and many African countries lack R&D and researcher data.
  • Composite indices depend on the choices and weights of their authors.
